# Password Attacks

## What it is
A password attack is when an attacker tries to break into your account by
guessing, cracking or stealing your password. They may use software that
tries thousands of combinations every second, or use passwords that were
leaked from other websites.

## Common types
- **Brute force:** trying every possible combination until one works
- **Dictionary attack:** trying common words and popular passwords
- **Credential stuffing:** using a leaked username and password from one site on many other sites
- **Guessing:** using personal details such as names, birthdays or pet names

## Example
You use "john1234" for your email, social media and online shopping. One
shop gets hacked and its customer passwords leak. Attackers try the same
password on your email and other accounts, and get in.

## Warning signs
- Login alerts from places or devices you don't recognise
- Password reset emails you didn't ask for
- Being locked out of your own account
- Friends receiving strange messages from your account

## How to protect yourself
- Use a long, unique password for every account (12 or more characters).
- Try a passphrase, such as four random words joined together.
- Turn on multi-factor authentication (MFA) wherever it's offered.
- Use a password manager so you don't have to remember everything.
- Never share your password, and avoid reusing it on other sites.

## What to do if you think you've been hacked
1. Change the password straight away, on that account and any others that use it.
2. Turn on MFA if it wasn't already on.
3. Log out of all other devices and check your account settings.
