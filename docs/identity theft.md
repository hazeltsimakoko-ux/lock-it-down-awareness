# Identity Theft

## What it is
Identity theft is when someone steals your personal information, such as
your ID number, bank details or date of birth, and uses it to pretend to be
you. They may use it to open accounts, take out loans or make purchases in
your name.

## How your details get stolen
- **Phishing:** fake messages that trick you into handing over details
- **Data breaches:** a company you use is hacked and customer data leaks
- **Discarded documents:** bank statements or ID copies thrown away without shredding
- **Oversharing:** posting personal details on social media
- **Stolen devices or mail:** phones, laptops or post that falls into the wrong hands

## Example
A thief finds your ID number and address in a discarded document. They use
these details to apply for a loan in your name. You only find out when you
receive a bill you don't recognise.

## Warning signs
- Bills or statements for accounts you never opened
- Unexpected debt collection calls or letters
- Transactions on your accounts that you don't recognise
- Being refused credit for no clear reason

## How to protect yourself
- Share personal details only when it is really necessary.
- Shred documents that contain personal information before throwing them away.
- Check your bank statements regularly.
- Use strong, unique passwords and turn on multi-factor authentication (MFA).
- Keep your social media privacy settings tight and avoid posting your ID number, address or date of birth.

## What to do if you think your identity was stolen
1. Contact your bank straight away and ask them to freeze or monitor your accounts.
2. Change the passwords on your important accounts.
3. Report it to the police.
4. Keep a record of what happened and when.
